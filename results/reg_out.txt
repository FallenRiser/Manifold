SECTION 2: Weight decay -- where it works, and where it does not
==========================================================================
Weight decay penalises large weights. Its effect is clearest when weight
MAGNITUDE is the thing that controls overfitting -- which is not always
the case. Three measurements make the honest picture.

2a) Regression, AdamW: the textbook bias-variance tradeoff.
    A wide net on 60 noisy points of a smooth 1-D target. As decay rises,
    train MSE goes UP (less fitting of noise) and test MSE goes DOWN.
    weight_decay   train_mse   test_mse
    ------------------------------------------
    0.0000         0.0221     0.0535
    0.0001         0.0220     0.0526
    0.0010         0.0216     0.0542
    0.0100         0.0218     0.0516
    0.0300         0.0224     0.0511
    0.1000         0.0227     0.0478
    0.3000         0.0290     0.0412

2b) Classification, SGD+momentum: decay closes the gap.
    The same overfitting classifier, trained with plain SGD (batch 32).
    weight_decay   train    test     gap
    ------------------------------------------
    0.0000         1.000   0.768   0.232
    0.0001         1.000   0.766   0.234
    0.0010         1.000   0.768   0.232
    0.0050         1.000   0.770   0.230
    0.0100         1.000   0.771   0.229
    0.0500         0.999   0.767   0.232

2c) Classification, full-batch AdamW: the honest null result.
    The SAME sweep under full-batch AdamW barely moves test accuracy --
    only ~400 steps, so per-step decay lr*wd*w never compounds enough,
    and the net memorises the 250 points with SMALL weights anyway.
    weight_decay   train    test     gap
    ------------------------------------------
    0.0000         1.000   0.755   0.245
    0.0100         1.000   0.751   0.249
    0.1000         1.000   0.753   0.247

==========================================================================
SECTION 3: Weight decay is not L2 (with Adam)
==========================================================================
For adaptive optimisers, decoupled weight decay (AdamW) and adding an
L2 term to the loss (Adam) are NOT the same update. Measured, same net,
same nominal strength 1e-3.

  AdamW decoupled wd=1e-3 :  train 1.000  test 0.751  gap 0.249
  Adam + L2 in loss 1e-3  :  train 1.000  test 0.770  gap 0.230
  (The two differ because Adam scales the L2 gradient by the per-parameter
   adaptive rate, while AdamW applies decay uniformly.)

==========================================================================
SECTION 5: Dropout at test time -- the scaling
==========================================================================
At test, dropout is turned OFF and activations are used at full strength
(inverted dropout already scaled them up during training). Using dropout
at test (MC-dropout) instead gives a NOISY, non-deterministic prediction.

  eval() deterministic test acc      : 0.749
  single MC-dropout pass (stochastic): mean 0.717 std 0.010  (varies run to run)
  50-pass MC-dropout average         : 0.749

==========================================================================
SECTION 6: Early stopping
==========================================================================
Track a held-out validation error while training; keep the weights at its
minimum. Early stopping is clearest when test error OVER-FITS OVER TIME --
bottoms out, then climbs as training continues.

6a) Regression: the clean overfitting-over-time curve.
    The wide net on 80 noisy points, 56 train / 24 val, Adam, 4000 epochs,
    tracking validation MSE. 5-seed average.
    best-val epoch (avg)   : 34 of 4000
    test MSE at best-val   : 0.0118
    test MSE at end        : 0.0760
    early stopping saves   : +0.0641 MSE

6b) Classification: stop on the RIGHT metric.
    On the overfitting classifier, validation LOSS and validation ACCURACY
    disagree: the loss bottoms almost immediately (the net gets over-
    confident on the label noise) while test accuracy keeps drifting up.
  best validation epoch (avg)   : 3 of 600
  test acc at best-val weights   : 0.741
  test acc after full 600 epochs : 0.754
  early stopping recovers        : -0.012

SECTION 8: Label smoothing and mixup
==========================================================================
Label smoothing softens the one-hot target; mixup trains on convex blends
of pairs of points and their labels. Both discourage overconfidence.

  method              train    test     gap
  ------------------------------------------------
  label_smooth=0.00     1.000   0.752   0.248
  label_smooth=0.05     1.000   0.752   0.248
  label_smooth=0.10     1.000   0.753   0.247
  label_smooth=0.20     1.000   0.754   0.246
  mixup alpha=0.20      1.000   0.757   0.243
  mixup alpha=0.40      1.000   0.749   0.251
  mixup alpha=1.00      1.000   0.747   0.253
